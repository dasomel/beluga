### 1. Known Problem & Trigger
* **Verified upstream** ([lima-vm/lima#5665](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/issues/5665)): Exact same bug reported on Lima/Colima (`vmType: vz`, 8 vCPUs, Ubuntu 24.04 arm64 kernel `6.8.0-64-generic`, Apple Silicon host awake with no `pmset` sleep/wake events, running k3s). Both `CLOCK_REALTIME` and `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` jumped $+3,002,405,619\text{ s}$ ($2^{56}/24\text{ MHz} = 95.14\text{ y}$) to Dec 2121, triggering false-positive RCU stall/NMI traces and endless `invalid argument` time-sync failures until `colima restart`.
* **Trigger**: [lima-vm/lima#5665](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/issues/5665) occurred under load while the host was awake, with no reliable reproducer; maintainer/author attributed it to a macOS `Virtualization.framework` / guest clocksource interaction. **Not found**: No upstream Apple `Virtualization.framework`, UTM, or KVM report was found isolating the exact trigger among P/E-core vCPU migration, high vCPU count, or macOS version (**inferred**: cross-vCPU `CNTVCT_EL0` skew under vCPU contention). Related: [lima-vm/lima#5543](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/issues/5543) (**verified upstream**) shows Lima's guestagent stepping `CLOCK_REALTIME` backward under host load, though `settimeofday` does not alter `CNTVCT_EL0`.

### 2. Kernel Mechanism & Newer Kernel Immunity
* **Verified upstream** ([v6.8 `timekeeping_internal.h`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/kernel/time/timekeeping_internal.h), [v6.8 `timekeeping.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/kernel/time/timekeeping.c), [v6.8 `arm_arch_timer.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/drivers/clocksource/arm_arch_timer.c)): `arch_sys_counter` uses `CLOCKSOURCE_MASK(56)` (`0xffffffffffffff`). In Linux 6.8, `CONFIG_CLOCKSOURCE_VALIDATE_LAST_CYCLE` (which zeroes deltas with the mask MSB set) is selected **only by `arch/x86/Kconfig`**. On `arm64`, `clocksource_delta(now, last, mask)` computes `(now - last) & mask`. When `now < last` by $\varepsilon$ cycles, unsigned underflow masked to 56 bits yields $2^{56} - \varepsilon$ cycles ($95.142\text{ y}$ at 24 MHz), which `timekeeping_advance()` / `timekeeping_forward_now()` add to both `CLOCK_REALTIME` and `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`.
* **Verified upstream** ([commit `c163e40af9b2`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/c163e40af9b2331b2c629fd4ec8b703ed4d4ae39.patch), [commit `76031d9536a0`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/76031d9536a076bf023bedbdb1b4317fc801dd67.patch), [commit `135225a363ae`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/135225a363ae.patch)): `135225a363ae` (v6.10) only guarded `timekeeping_cycles_to_ns()` read paths. Thomas Gleixner's `"timekeeping: Always check for negative motion"` (`c163e40af9b2`, refined by `76031d9536a0` to clamp deltas $>87.5\%$ of `mask` to `0` in `clocksource_delta()`) landed in **Linux 6.13**, making guest kernels $\ge 6.13$ immune.

### 3. Why `systemd-timesyncd`, `chronyd`, and `date -s` Fail with `EINVAL`
* **Verified upstream** ([`timesyncd-manager.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/src/timesync/timesyncd-manager.c), [v6.8 `timekeeping.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/kernel/time/timekeeping.c), [v6.8 `time64.h`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/include/linux/time64.h)): `systemd-timesyncd` calls `clock_adjtime(CLOCK_REALTIME, &tmx)` with `ADJ_SETOFFSET`. `do_adjtimex()` $\to$ `timekeeping_inject_offset(&delta)` checks `timespec64_compare(&tk->wall_to_monotonic, ts) > 0 || !timespec64_valid_settod(&tmp)`. The 2026 target passes `timespec64_valid_settod()`, but **fails `wall_to_monotonic > ts`**: `wall_to_monotonic` (`monotonic - realtime`, negated boot epoch) remains $\approx -1.79\times 10^9\text{ s}$ ($-56.9\text{ y}$), while the offset `ts` is $\approx -3.0024\times 10^9\text{ s}$ ($-95.14\text{ y}$). Stepping `CLOCK_REALTIME` to 2026 while `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` is 95.14 years would place the system's calculated boot timestamp (`realtime - monotonic`) in ~1931 (pre-1970 Unix epoch).
* **Verified upstream** ([v6.8 `timekeeping.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/kernel/time/timekeeping.c), [lima-vm/lima#5665](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/issues/5665)): `do_settimeofday64()` enforces the **identical** `timespec64_compare(&tk->wall_to_monotonic, &ts_delta) > 0` check. Therefore **`chronyd` (`makestep`), `settimeofday`, `clock_settime`, `date -s`, `hwclock --hctosys`, and `lima-guestagent` (`unix.Settimeofday`) all fail with `EINVAL`**.

### 4. Mitigations for Lima/Colima `vz` Guests
* **Verified upstream**:
  * **Guest kernel $\ge 6.13$**: Prevents the wrap in `clocksource_delta()` ([`c163e40af9b2`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/c163e40af9b2331b2c629fd4ec8b703ed4d4ae39.patch)).
  * **Guest time-sync tools**: Lima $\ge 2.1.0$ already syncs guest time every 10 s via `Settimeofday` ([lima-vm/lima#4527](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/pull/4527), [`timesync.go`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lima-vm/lima/master/pkg/hostagent/timesync.go)), but neither it, `chrony` (`makestep 1 -1`), nor `hwclock --hctosys` can prevent counter underflow or bypass `do_settimeofday64()`'s `EINVAL` after a jump.
  * **Kernel cmdline**: `arm64.nowfi` does **not** exist (`nohlt` disables `wfi`, [v6.8 `kernel-parameters.txt`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/Documentation/admin-guide/kernel-parameters.txt)); no alternative hardware clocksource exists on arm64 `vz`.
* **Inferred (speculation, not documented upstream)**: Reducing vCPUs, `nohz=off`/`nohlt`, or switching `vmType: qemu`.

### 5. Post-Jump Misbehavior & Reboot Necessity
* **Verified upstream** ([lima-vm/lima#5665](https://github.com/lima-vm/lima/issues/5665), [v6.8 `timekeeping.c`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/kernel/time/timekeeping.c)): **A reboot is mandatory** because `CLOCK_REALTIME` cannot even be set back to 2026 while `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` is at 95 years. Furthermore, TLS clients fail (`x509: certificate has expired or is not yet valid`), and `systemd-timesyncd` saves the 2122 timestamp to disk and restores it on the next boot until hostagent/NTP steps it back. **Inferred**: Even if wall time were stepped back, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`/`CLOCK_BOOTTIME` stay at +95 years, stranding future-armed realtime timers, k3s/Kubernetes `Lease` `renewTime`s, etcd/kine records, certs minted in 2122, and `journald` ordering.
